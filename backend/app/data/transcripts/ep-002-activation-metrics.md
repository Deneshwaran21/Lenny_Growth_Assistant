# Finding Your Activation Metric

Most teams pick an activation metric too early and never revisit it. A
good activation metric should correlate strongly with long-term retention,
be reachable within a single session or two, and be simple enough that
everyone in the company can say it out loud without checking a dashboard.

One useful exercise is to take your most retained cohort of users and work
backward: what did they all do in their first week that churned users did
not do? This is more reliable than guessing at a metric from a product
roadmap document. For a collaboration tool, that might be "invited a
second teammate." For an analytics tool, it might be "connected a real
data source and viewed a chart with their own data in it," not just
completed a tutorial.

It's also worth separating "aha moment" from "habit moment." The aha
moment is the first spark of realized value. The habit moment is when the
behavior becomes routine enough that removing the product would be
noticeable and painful. Growth teams often over-index on driving people to
the aha moment and under-invest in the habit loop that follows — reminder
emails, saved work, recurring triggers — which is usually where the real
retention gains are hiding.

When measuring activation, resist the urge to average across all user
segments. A self-serve individual user and a team admin onboarding five
colleagues have completely different activation paths, and blending them
into one metric usually produces a number nobody trusts or acts on.
