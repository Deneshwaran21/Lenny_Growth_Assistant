# Pricing and Packaging for Growth

Pricing is a growth lever, not just a finance decision. Packaging — how
you bundle features into tiers — often has a bigger effect on conversion
and expansion than the actual price points. Teams frequently ship a
pricing page that reflects the org chart of the engineering team rather
than the actual jobs customers are trying to do.

A useful framework is to separate "value metric" from "vanity metric."
The value metric is the unit that scales with the customer's perceived
value — seats, usage volume, workflows automated — and should be the axis
your pricing scales on. A vanity metric might correlate with usage but
doesn't map to value the customer actually feels, and charging on it tends
to create resentment and churn risk at renewal.

When introducing a new pricing tier, grandfather existing customers
carefully and communicate the change well in advance. Silent price
increases are one of the fastest ways to spike churn and support tickets
simultaneously. It's also worth running the new pricing past a small
segment of friendly customers before a full rollout — pricing feedback
from people who already love the product is far more actionable than
feedback from the general market.

Finally, packaging free and paid tiers requires deciding what stays free
forever versus what is time- or usage-limited. Features that drive
network effects or invite collaborators (like sharing or commenting) are
often worth keeping free indefinitely, because they expand your top of
funnel even if the inviting user never pays.
