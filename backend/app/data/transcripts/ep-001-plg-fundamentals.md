# Product-Led Growth Fundamentals

Product-led growth (PLG) works best when the product itself is the primary
driver of acquisition, conversion, and expansion, rather than sales or
marketing motions bolted on afterward. The core idea is that the product
should deliver value to the user before you ask for anything in return —
a credit card, a sales call, or even an email address, if you can help it.

A common mistake teams make is treating PLG as a pricing model rather than
a distribution strategy. Freemium and free trials are tactics, not the
strategy itself. The real strategy question is: what is the smallest unit
of value a new user can experience, and how fast can they reach it? Teams
that obsess over "time to value" and shrink it aggressively tend to see
much stronger activation numbers than teams that only tweak onboarding
copy.

Another important idea is the distinction between activation and
retention. Activation answers "did the user experience the core value at
least once," while retention answers "do they keep coming back for that
value." A product can have great activation and terrible retention if the
core value is a one-time novelty rather than a recurring need. Before
investing heavily in top-of-funnel acquisition, it's worth confirming the
retention curve actually flattens — otherwise you are just paying to fill
a leaky bucket.

Finally, PLG does not mean "no sales team." Many of the most successful
PLG companies layer a sales-assisted motion on top of self-serve once
usage signals show a team or account is ready to expand. The product
becomes the top-of-funnel and qualification engine, and sales becomes an
expansion and enterprise-readiness function rather than the primary
acquisition channel.
